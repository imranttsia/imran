# Essay

Use this skill when the user asks for an essay on a topic (e.g. "Write me an essay on X"). It reproduces the style of the reference essay "Artificial Intelligence: A Tool That Reflects Its Makers".

## Defaults (use unless the user says otherwise)
- Length: about 450-600 words, 5-6 paragraphs.
- Format: title plus continuous prose. No bullet points, no sub-headers, no bold inside the body.
- Tone: clear, confident, balanced, and accessible to an educated general reader. Plain words over jargon. Vary sentence length.
- Language: the language the user wrote in.
- Do not ask clarifying questions for a simple request; pick the most sensible angle and write. Offer adjustments at the end.

## Structure
1. **Title**: format "Topic: A Memorable Claim" (e.g. "A Tool That Reflects Its Makers"). The subtitle should state the essay's central idea.
2. **Introduction (1 paragraph)**: Show how the topic has entered everyday life with 3-4 concrete examples, then state the thesis: the topic's outcome depends on human choices, not on the thing itself.
3. **The case for (1 paragraph)**: Concrete benefits across several domains (e.g. health, science, industry, education). Give one specific example per domain and end with the underlying value (what it frees people to do).
4. **The risks (1 paragraph)**: Real concerns stated fairly: bias, misinformation, jobs/inequality, privacy, concentration of power, and any longer-term worry. Be specific, not alarmist.
5. **The path forward (1 paragraph)**: Open with "None of this is inevitable" or similar. Give 3-4 practical commitments split by actor (builders/developers, governments, schools/employers, individual users).
6. **Limits or counterpoint (1 short paragraph)**: Clarify what the topic is not or cannot do, and why human responsibility remains.
7. **Conclusion (1 paragraph)**: Reframe the question (not "good or bad" but "who shapes it and for whose benefit"), give the hopeful and cautionary outcomes, and end on a short, memorable line about responsibility.

## Style rules
- Every claim should be concrete: name specific domains, mechanisms or examples.
- Present both sides fairly; never be purely promotional or purely doom-laden.
- Echo the thesis in the conclusion with fresh wording, not copy-paste.
- Avoid clichés ("in today's fast-paced world", "since the dawn of time"), filler transitions, and rhetorical questions in the body.
- Do not invent statistics, quotes or citations. If facts or figures are needed and web search is available, search first and cite sources.
- Adapt the domains and actors in steps 3-5 to the topic; the shape stays the same even when the subject is not technology (e.g. climate, social media, education, a historical event).

## Adjustments
- If the user gives a word count, grade level, angle, or purpose (school, blog, speech), follow it and scale paragraph count accordingly.
- For academic use, add a short "Sources" list only if real sources were actually retrieved.

## Finish
End the reply with one short line offering to change length, level or angle, or to turn the essay into a Word document. Do not recap the steps taken.
