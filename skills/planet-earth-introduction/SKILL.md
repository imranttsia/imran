# Planet Earth Introduction

Write a clear, engaging introduction to Planet Earth in flowing prose (no bullet points, no headers unless the user asks). Default length is about 450 words in 6 paragraphs. Adjust length, tone, and reading level if the user specifies (for example: school students, general audience, technical readers, 100-word summary, 1000-word essay).

## Structure (one paragraph each, in this order)

1. **Identity and origin**: Third planet from the Sun, the only known place with life. Formed about 4.54 billion years ago from gas and dust around the young Sun. Diameter about 12,742 km, average distance from the Sun about 150 million km, within the habitable zone where liquid water can exist.
2. **Water and surface**: Oceans cover about 71% of the surface, hence the nickname the Blue Planet. Land makes up about 29%. Continents shift through plate tectonics, which builds mountains, causes earthquakes and volcanism, and recycles carbon.
3. **Internal structure and atmosphere**: Crust, convecting mantle, liquid outer core, solid iron-nickel inner core. The outer core generates the magnetic field that shields against solar radiation. Atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen; it supports breathing, regulates temperature via the greenhouse effect, and the ozone layer protects life.
4. **Life**: Life emerged at least 3.5 billion years ago and diversified into millions of species. Life transformed the planet in turn: photosynthesis oxygenated the atmosphere, and living systems cycle carbon, water, and nutrients.
5. **Moon, rotation, and orbit**: One natural satellite, the Moon, stabilizes the roughly 23.5 degree axial tilt (seasons) and drives tides. Rotation takes about 24 hours (day and night); one orbit takes about 365.25 days.
6. **Humans and the future**: More than 8 billion people. Human activity (agriculture, industry, energy, urbanization) is now a major force shaping the planet, bringing climate change, biodiversity loss, and resource pressure. Close with a thoughtful line about understanding Earth's systems and living within their limits.

## Style rules

- Open with a strong, factual first sentence that states what Earth is.
- Use precise figures, rounded sensibly with words like "about" or "roughly".
- Keep a neutral, informative, slightly inspiring tone; avoid hype and clichés.
- Make each paragraph flow into the next; end with a reflective closing sentence.
- Do not invent statistics. If unsure of a figure, state it approximately or omit it.

## Variations

- **Shorter** (50 to 150 words): keep paragraphs 1, 2, 4, and a one-sentence close.
- **Longer** (700+ words): expand each section with examples (for example specific tectonic features, atmospheric layers, major biomes).
- **Younger audience**: simpler vocabulary, one vivid analogy per paragraph.
- **Other planets or topics**: reuse the same six-part arc (identity, surface, structure, special features, companions and motion, outlook).
- **Output format**: deliver as plain text by default. If the user asks for a document, slide, or file, produce it in the requested format with the same content.
